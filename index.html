02 · Current research · UC Santa Cruz AIEA Lab

Teaching a broken robot to keep walking.

Fault Recovery in RL Locomotion Through Online Residual Adaptation — can a small correction module, updated during execution, restore a quadruped's gait after a hardware fault without retraining the policy underneath?

Nominal gait
base policy · healthy
Fwd vel0.50 m/s
Step000
Control60 Hz

01 — Premise

Policies are trained on robots that never break.

In the real world motors weaken, joints seize, and sensors start reporting values that aren't true. A locomotion policy trained only under ideal conditions has never once been shown what failure feels like — so it has no response to it.

02 — Apparatus

A quadruped you can break on purpose.

Twelve revolute joints in PyBullet, physics at 240 Hz stepped down to 60 Hz control. A PPO policy trained across five seeds, each gated on a numerical gait check before it's allowed into the experiment.

34-dim observation12-dim action0.5 m/s targetCPU only

03 — Injection

At step 200, something fails.

Five fault families, injected mid-rollout on a matched seed set so every fault type is compared against identical initial conditions. Simulation makes it possible to test failures that would be reckless to induce on real hardware.

torque_limitjoint_lockactuation_delaysensor_dropoutsensor_noise

04 — Method

Correct around the fault, don't retrain through it.

A small residual module sits between the frozen base policy and the actuators, updating online. The seized joint stays seized — the correction redistributes the gait around it. Measured against two bounds: no adaptation, and full retraining.

In progress — the residual module is the current build target.

05 — Finding

The first real result was about the measurement.

The unadapted baseline "recovers" in 100% of trials on all five faults — a ceiling, not a success. With no headroom, no adaptation method could demonstrate an improvement. torque_limit scores 100% recovery and a 36.5% fall rate at the same time, which is only possible because recovery was scored as a momentary velocity crossing rather than a sustained return to gait.

Fixing the metric before scaling the experiment.

A uniform 100% is the finding: the recovery criterion fires on transient velocity noise within 2–10 control steps, so it credits recoveries that never happened. Severities go up and the criterion gets a sustained window before this baseline can mean anything.
